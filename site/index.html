    Method

    Boundaries and split counties

    The map uses the Census Bureau's cartographic boundary files for the 119th Congress. Five counties are divided between districts. Their shapes show only the part inside the district, and population shares come from the 2020 Census tabulated by county part. County-level statistics such as income always describe the whole county and are labeled that way.

    Survey estimates and margins of error

    Most figures about people and homes come from the American Community Survey, a sample survey. District figures use 1-year estimates; county figures use 5-year estimates, which are more reliable for small counties. Margins of error at 90 percent confidence appear beside district figures. Small differences may not be meaningful.

    Following the seat

    Roll-call votes are attributed to whoever held the seat on the date of the vote. Party agreement and winning-side rates use only yes-or-no votes; quorum calls and the election of the Speaker are left out. Definitions appear beside every rate.

    Unofficial and scheduled information

    Some facts, like a race called by news organizations before certification, are not yet in any official dataset. Those appear as notes labeled unofficial, with sources, a verification date, and a review-by date. The cross-checks flag any note past its review date.

    What this atlas does not do

    It does not rate, endorse, or characterize candidates, officeholders, or parties, and it does not predict elections. Party colors appear only in election charts.
